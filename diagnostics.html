Diagnostics

How well the models fit

A z-score is only trustworthy if, in healthy children, it behaves like a standard normal variable at every size. These checks show whether the median, spread and shape of each model hold up across height, age and build.

z-scores against body size:

Each panel splits the reference children into 20 equal-sized groups (about 160 each) along one variable. Each shaded block spans its group's range on that variable and shows the middle 50% and 80% of z; wide blocks mean sparse data, such as the heaviest children. Dots are group means at the group median, and the dark line is a smoothed trend (1st to 99th percentile) with ±2 SE. For a well-fitting model the trend stays near 0 and the blocks stay level near ±0.67 and ±1.28. Hover a group for its numbers.

Q-tests by height group:

Royston–Wright tests, the standard check for growth references. Within each height group, Z1 tests the mean (median model), Z2 the variance (σ), Z3 the skewness (λ) and Z4 the kurtosis. Values beyond ±2 are highlighted, as are overall p-values below 0.05. The Z1 test subtracts the degrees of freedom of the whole median model, including the weight-for-height and age terms, so its p-value is conservative.

Worm plots by height group:

A worm plot is a Q-Q plot with the diagonal removed, drawn separately for six height groups. A flat worm inside the grey band means the median, spread and shape are all right for children of that size. A worm that tilts suggests the spread is off; a U or S shape suggests skewness or tail weight. Points outside the band are marked in red.

Shape comparison

How diameter scales with height

The local height exponent, d ln(diameter) / d ln(height), from a smooth fit, with ±2 SE bands. An exponent of 1 means the aorta grows in proportion to height; adult data are often closer to 0.5. A single fixed ratio index assumes one exponent at every size.
