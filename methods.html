Every method on the same healthy children

Each published way of normalizing aortic diameters, applied to the same 3,200 healthy children. A good reference should give z-scores with mean 0 and SD 1, about 2.3% beyond each of ±2, and no trend left against height, age, build or sex.

What was compared

Every method below was applied to the healthy children of the PHN public database (after the same cleaning as the model). This model, cross-validated scores each child with a model fitted without them (10 folds), so it has no home advantage. The PHN, Mahgerefteh and CSA/height methods were derived from these same children, so they do. Lauzon-Schnittka is the only reference built on a different cohort (a Canadian multicentre clinical cohort read by many readers), computed with the authors' own R function; it tests how well a reference transfers to another lab. Mahgerefteh is published for ages 2 and up and is shown by its height index, the paper's main contribution; its BSA0.5 index is left out because it is the PHN BSA index with nearly identical values (2.07 vs 2.06 cm/m at the sinuses, 1.78 vs 1.79 at the ascending aorta). Chaudhary CSA/height covers the sinuses and ascending aorta only.

All figures are group summaries (whole-cohort statistics and 20 equal-count groups of about 160 children); no individual records are published. Comparison generated —.

What it shows

  • This model holds up out of sample. Cross-validated z-scores keep SD 1.01, 2.1–2.7% beyond each of ±2 and no trend against height, age, build, BMIz or sex. The sinotubular junction has slightly heavy tails.
  • Ignoring weight costs about 0.2 z per SD of build (height-and-sex model), and changes about 1 in 4 of the z > 2 calls.
  • BSA0.5 indices overcorrect for weight (heavier children score about 0.1–0.15 z lower per SD of build), are right-skewed, and leave boys about 0.1–0.5 z above girls.
  • Dividing by height overcorrects for height (Mahgerefteh: taller children score about 0.2–0.3 z lower per SD), because the aorta grows less than in proportion to height.
  • Lauzon-Schnittka transfers in shape but not in level. Its trends against body size are nearly flat in these children, but the spread is narrower (SD about 0.9 at the sinuses and junction) and its ascending aorta reference sits about half a z-score higher than these measurements, so it flags only a third of the children this model places above +2. A different measurement convention or reader population is a likely reason.
  • CSA/height is right-skewed (skewness about 0.5): 3–4% of healthy children land above +2 and under 1% below −2, with trends left against build and BMIz. The continuous version as distributed is also offset (sinuses −0.5 z; ascending aorta +0.3 z, flagging 6.5% of healthy children).
  • Extra weight raises the expected diameter only a little: at BMIz +3, about 2–3.5% in this model and 3–5% for Lauzon-Schnittka at the sinuses and ascending aorta, against 7–17% implied by BSA0.5.

Where each method drifts:

Mean z (or its SD) in 20 equal-count groups along each variable. A good reference stays flat at 0 (or at 1 for SD). Choose methods with the buttons; hover a chart for the values in each group.

Do the methods flag the same children?

Every child here is healthy, so above +2 does not mean dilated: about 2.3% of any healthy group should land there (the tick on each bar, roughly 73 children). A good method flags about that many, and ideally the same children as other good methods. Each bar is the set of children a method places above +2, split by whether this model (cross-validated) places them there too. The hollow segment shows children this model flags that the method does not.
